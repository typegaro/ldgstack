# Unslop

Write it clean the first time; a cleanup pass misses what a careful draft avoids. Then reread once for these.

## Cut

1. Openers and closers that say nothing: "Great question", "Certainly", "I hope this helps", "In summary", "Let me know if".
2. Hedges and intensifiers: "very", "really", "quite", "essentially", "basically", "actually", "it's worth noting", "importantly".
3. Inflated words where a plain one works: leverage (use), utilize (use), facilitate (help), robust, seamless, comprehensive, cutting-edge, delve, crucial, pivotal, landscape, tapestry, journey.
4. Lists of three that exist for rhythm, not content. Keep the items that carry information.
5. A summary that repeats what was just said.
6. Bold on ordinary words, headers on two-paragraph texts, emoji, exclamation marks.
7. Long dashes used to stitch clauses together, and colons used as mid-sentence connectors. Split the sentence instead.
8. "Not only X but also Y", "It's not X, it's Y", and other set pieces.
9. Vague claims without a subject or evidence ("this improves performance"). Say what, by how much, measured how, or cut it.
10. Passive voice that hides who did it, when who matters.

## Keep

Every fact, number, decision, open question, and caveat. Shorter is the goal only after nothing is lost. Technical terms the reader knows stay; jargon the reader doesn't gets a plain word.

## Check

Read it aloud in your head. If a sentence would sound odd said to a colleague, rewrite it.
